Hevy vs Strong: Which Lifting App Should You Actually Use?

Updated July 2026 · by the Monolift team

Hevy vs Strong is the most common matchup in lifting apps, and for good reason: they are the two best structured workout loggers you can put on a phone. We build a competing app, so read this knowing that — but the honest answer is that both are good, they win for different people, and the real decision comes down to how you like to train and log.

  • Pick Hevy if you want the more generous free tier, a social feed, and apps on iOS, Android, and the web.
  • Pick Strong if you want a quieter, simpler app with no feed and the option to pay once for a lifetime license.
  • Feature-wise they are close. The split is social and cross-platform (Hevy) versus simple and pay-once (Strong).
  • If your actual complaint is that logging sets feels slow in both, there's a third option below.

What Hevy does well

Hevy is the newer of the two and it shows in the polish. The routine builder is fast, the exercise library is large, per-exercise charts are solid, and it runs on iOS, Android, and a proper web app — if you train with an Android friend or like reviewing your log on a laptop, that matters.

Its two standout advantages: the free tier and the feed. Free users can log unlimited workouts, with caps on routines and custom exercises rather than on logging itself, which is why Hevy usually wins "best free tracker" discussions. The social feed — follow friends, see their sessions, leave likes — is genuinely motivating for some people and genuinely annoying for others. You know which one you are.

If the feed is the part that puts you off, we've covered quieter options in Hevy alternatives.

What Strong does well

Strong is the veteran. It has been the default iOS lifting log for years and it earns that by staying out of your way: no feed, no gamification, no clutter. You open it, you log sets in a clean structured interface, you leave. The plate calculator and warm-up tools are small touches lifters actually use.

Its other big draw is the pricing model: Strong still sells a one-time lifetime purchase, which is rare in 2026. If you plan to log lifts for the next decade and hate subscriptions on principle, that alone can settle the argument. The free version lets you log without limits but caps you at three routines, which is the pinch point that pushes most people to pay.

If Strong's development pace or routine cap bothers you, see Strong app alternatives.

Hevy or Strong app: what they cost

As of mid-2026, on the US App Store: Hevy Pro runs about $2.99 a month, $23.99 a year, or $74.99 lifetime — though Hevy's pricing has shifted during 2026, so check the current listing. Strong Pro runs about $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year, with a $99.99 one-time lifetime option. Both are cheap relative to a gym membership; neither price should be the deciding factor.

Is Hevy Pro worth it?

For most lifters, not immediately — the free tier covers unlimited logging, and you only feel the walls when you outgrow the routine and custom-exercise caps or want your full training history unlocked. If you run several programs at once or log unusual exercises, Pro pays for itself. If you run one program and log the basics, stay free until you hit a wall.

Is the Strong app worth it?

The free version is a real logger, not a demo — unlimited workouts, three routines. If three routines is enough, you may never pay. If you upgrade, the lifetime purchase is the rational buy for anyone who expects to still be lifting in five years. The honest caveat: you're buying an app that changes slowly. Some people call that stability; some call it stagnation.

What the Hevy vs Strong Reddit threads actually say

If you scan the recurring Reddit threads on this matchup, the consensus is more settled than you'd expect. Strong is the default answer in powerlifting and strength-focused communities, where simplicity and longevity win. Hevy is the default in general fitness threads, where the free tier and the social feed win. And the most repeated advice on both sides is the same: start free, and only pay when you hit a specific limit that annoys you.

What you won't find is a knockout. Nobody credibly claims one app is bad. They're two good implementations of the same idea: a structured logger where every set is a row with a weight field and a reps field.

The trade-off both apps share: logging is still tapping

Here's the thing the head-to-head threads skip. Hevy and Strong share an interaction model: pick the exercise from a list, tap the weight field, type on the keyboard, tap the reps field, type again, tap the checkmark. It's fine at a desk. Mid-workout, with 90 seconds between sets and chalk on your hands, it's enough friction that plenty of experienced lifters quietly go back to a paper notebook or an Apple Notes workout template — fast to write in, useless for progress tracking.

Monolift is our attempt to keep the notes-speed and add the tracking. A workout is just a plain-text note:

Bench Press: 135 lbs 8, 8, 6
Incline DB Press: 60 lbs 10, 10, 9
Cable Fly: 45 lbs 12, 12

Type it like that and it live-renders into cards with tappable weight and rep pills — colon marks the exercise, commas separate sets. Between sets you don't type at all: long-press any number and slide to change it. About two seconds a set, no keyboard. You still get per-exercise charts, automatic PR detection, and rest timers on your Lock Screen, so tracking progressive overload doesn't cost you the logging speed.

In Monolift Moving over takes minutes, not an import tool: paste or type your current program as plain text ("Squat: 225 lbs 5, 5, 5") and it becomes your log. Next session, long-press the weight, slide to today's number, done. There's no automatic Hevy or Strong history import — if years of in-app history matter to you, that's a real switching cost to weigh.

To be equally honest about the other direction: Monolift is iPhone-only (iOS 17+), English-only, has no Android or web version, and no Apple Watch app — Hevy and Strong both have Watch apps. There's no public social feed either; Squads are small private crews for sharing workouts and kudos, not a follower feed. If cross-platform or public social is a must, Hevy is your answer and we'll say so plainly.

The bottom line

Choosing between Hevy or Strong, you can't really lose: Hevy for the free tier, feed, and cross-platform reach; Strong for the quiet interface and the pay-once option. Try both free versions for a week each — that settles it faster than any article.

But if you tried a structured logger before and drifted back to Notes or paper because the tapping wore you down, the problem was never Hevy vs Strong — it was the form-filling itself.
