The Best Hevy Alternatives for Serious Lifters

Updated July 2026 · by the Monolift team

Hevy is a genuinely good app, so if you're hunting for a Hevy alternative it's usually not because it's broken. It's because something about it rubs you wrong: the social feed you never asked for, the tap-heavy routine screens, the free-tier caps, or the feeling that logging a set takes more screen time than it should. We build a workout tracker ourselves, so we're biased — but we've used every app on this list for real training weeks, and we'll tell you plainly where each one beats us.

Short answer: the best Hevy alternative depends on what pushed you away. If you want the fastest possible logging with no feed, get Monolift (iPhone only). If you want a near-identical minimal logger, get Strong. If you want proven programs for free, get Boostcamp. If you want workouts generated for you, get Fitbod.

Why lifters go looking for a Hevy alternative

Give Hevy its due first: it's polished, it runs on iOS, Android, and the web, it has an Apple Watch app, and Hevy Pro is one of the cheapest upgrades in the category — with a lifetime option, as of mid-2026. If price is your main criterion, honestly, stay on Hevy.

The complaints we hear are different. The free tier caps your routines and custom exercises. The social feed turns a logbook into another app that wants your attention. And the logging flow itself — pick routine, open exercise, tap field, type number, keyboard up, keyboard down, repeat — is fine, but it's not fast. If you've ever thought "I could do this quicker in Apple Notes," you're the person this page is for (and our Apple Notes workout template guide is proof people really do that).

Monolift — best Hevy alternative for iPhone users who want speed

Monolift is our app, so calibrate accordingly. It exists because of one specific frustration: every mainstream tracker makes you tap through screens and keyboards to record a number you already know. Monolift treats a workout as a plain-text note. You type it the way you'd scribble it on paper, and it live-renders into cards with tappable weight and rep pills:

Bench Press: 185 lbs 8, 8, 6
Incline DB Press: 70 lbs 10, 10, 9
Cable Fly: 42.5 lbs 12, 12

The part that replaces Hevy's keyboard entirely is drag-to-dial: long-press any number and slide to change it — about two seconds a set, one thumb, no keyboard. Plate mode steps through real plate loads with a haptic tick, which sounds like a gimmick until you're adjusting between sets with chalk on your hands.

In Monolift Moving off Hevy takes minutes, not an import wizard: open a new note and type your program as plain text — one line per exercise, colon after the name, commas between sets. It renders into cards instantly. From there, drag-to-dial handles every weight change, one-tap add set handles extra volume, and PR detection and per-exercise charts run automatically. There's no automated Hevy import, so a 5-exercise day is genuinely a couple of minutes of typing.

You still get the tracker essentials: rest timers as Lock Screen Live Activities (with an alarm that buzzes even when the app is closed), per-exercise progress charts, automatic weight and rep PR detection, and one-handed mode. Pro (fully included in the 7-day trial) adds an AI coach that reads your actual training history — it can build or edit a workout, flag plateaus, and suggest deloads, which we cover in when to deload. Instead of Hevy's public feed there are private Squads: your crew, shared workouts, kudos, a group streak — plus Banaño, a pixel gym monkey who ranks up as you log. Seasoning, not the sales pitch; more on that in our gamified workout apps guide.

The honest trade-offs: iPhone-only — no Android, no web, no Apple Watch app — English-only, and after the 7-day trial logging requires a subscription (the App Store shows pricing). Hevy Pro costs less. If any of those are dealbreakers, one of the next three apps is your answer.

Strong — the closest like-for-like replacement

Strong is the app Hevy was largely modeled on: a clean, routine-based set logger with a plate calculator, warm-up sets, and a proper Apple Watch app. If you like how Hevy works and just want out of its ecosystem, Strong is the smallest possible change. The free version is unlimited logging but capped at three routines; the paid unlock has long included a one-time lifetime purchase, which as of mid-2026 still shows on its listing and is something Hevy switchers specifically hunt for. The common knock is pace: development moves slowly, and the design has changed little in years — which is either a bug or a feature depending on your temperament. We compare the two head-to-head in Hevy vs Strong, and if you end up wanting off Strong too, there's a separate guide to Strong alternatives.

Boostcamp — best free option if you want a program handed to you

Boostcamp's pitch is different: it's less a blank logger and more a library of coach-written programs — 5/3/1, GZCLP, and thousands more — with tracking built in. As of mid-2026 the program library and core tracking are free, which makes it the strongest genuinely-free Hevy alternative on this list. The trade-off is that logging happens inside a program's structure; if you train freestyle or change plans mid-session, it fights you a little. Great for someone who wants to stop programming and just follow something proven.

Fitbod — best if you don't want to think about programming at all

Fitbod generates each workout for you based on the muscle groups you've hit recently and the equipment you have. It's the right pick for someone who was using Hevy's routines reluctantly and really just wants to be told what to do today. Two caveats: it's effectively subscription-only (the free version is a short trial, not a tier), and experienced lifters often override its selections enough that they'd be better off with a real program on Boostcamp or a fast blank logger. It solves a different problem than Hevy does — know which problem you have.

Also searching for a Jefit alternative?

A lot of Hevy-alternative searches are really Jefit-refugee searches, so: Jefit's strength is its enormous exercise database and long feature list, but the free version has grown heavy with ads, and the interface stacks features on features. If bloat is why you're leaving, don't lateral into another everything-app — you'll be back here in a year. The whole point of a Jefit alternative should be subtraction: Strong if you want minimal-and-familiar, Monolift if you want minimal-and-fast. How to move your training history out of Hevy

Hevy lets you export your workout history as a CSV from the profile settings — do that first regardless of where you land, so your data isn't stranded in an app you've stopped opening. Then be realistic about what transfers: no app on this list magically ingests a Hevy CSV into a native history, and we won't pretend Monolift does either. What actually matters day one is your current program and your current working weights, and retyping those takes minutes anywhere. Keep the CSV as your archive; if tracking progressive overload is the goal, your next PR matters more than your 2024 history.

In Monolift If your pre-Hevy history lives in a paper notebook, there's one shortcut: photo import (Pro, included in the trial). Snap a photo of the page and Monolift turns it into a formatted workout note. For a Hevy CSV, the practical route is pasting your program in as plain text — the auto-formatter cleans up messy text into proper exercise cards.

Bottom line: stay on Hevy if cheap Pro, Android, or the Watch app matter to you — that's the honest call. Switch to Strong for the same experience with a lifetime price, Boostcamp for free proven programs, Fitbod for auto-generated sessions. And if the real reason you searched "hevy alternative" is that logging feels slower than lifting, that's the exact itch Monolift was built to scratch: a plain-text log with drag-to-dial speed, no feed, on the phone already in your pocket.

If Hevy's screens and feed slow you down more than they help, log one real session in Monolift — the 7-day trial includes everything, so you'll know by Friday.
