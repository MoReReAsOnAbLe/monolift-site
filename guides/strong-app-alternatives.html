The Best Strong App Alternatives for Lifters on iPhone

Updated July 2026 · by the Monolift team

Strong has been the default iPhone lifting log for over a decade, and it earned that. But if you're reading this, something stopped working — the subscription direction, the tap-tap-keyboard logging flow, or the sense that a simple log kept growing in directions you never asked for. Picking a Strong app alternative in 2026 comes down to two jobs: get your training history out safely, then choose the logging style you actually want to live with. Here's both, honestly.

Short answer: export your data first — in Strong, go to Settings → Export Strong Data and save the CSV somewhere safe. Then pick by logging style: Monolift for the fastest plain-text logging on iPhone, Hevy for the closest like-for-like replacement (it imports Strong CSVs), StrengthLog for a generous free tier, or Boostcamp if you'd rather follow a coach-written program.

Why lifters go looking for a Strong app alternative

Fairness first, because most switchers don't hate Strong. It's clean, stable, has a long track record, and it still sells a one-time lifetime unlock alongside its subscription — genuinely rare in 2026. If it still works for you, keep it.

Read any recent Strong app review thread, though, and the complaints cluster in the same places. Pricing has moved around over the years — early adopters remember a cheap one-time unlock, and as of mid-2026 the model is subscription-first with a pricier lifetime tier, which left some long-time users feeling burned. The pace of new features gets mentioned. And then there's the one that matters most day to day: the logging flow itself. Strong logs the way most trackers do — tap an exercise from a list, tap a field, type a weight on the keyboard, tap a checkmark, repeat. It's fine. It's also a lot of taps to write down three numbers between sets.

None of that dealbreaking? Strong still holds a spot on our best workout tracker apps for iPhone list. This guide is for everyone who's done.

How to export Strong app workout data (do this first)

Before you try anything new, get your history out. It takes about two minutes:

  1. Open Strong and go to your profile, then Settings.
  2. Tap Export Strong Data.
  3. Strong generates a CSV file — save it to Files, email it to yourself, or drop it in a cloud drive.

The CSV holds your workout dates, workout and exercise names, every set's weight and reps, and your notes. Two things worth knowing: as of mid-2026, Strong can't re-import its own CSV, so this file is an archive, not a restore point — and the menu occasionally shifts between versions, so if you don't see the option, search "export" in Strong's help pages. Do the export even if you're not sure you're leaving. Years of training data shouldn't exist only inside one app.

1. Monolift — logging faster than paper

Monolift's pitch is the opposite of app bloat: a workout is a plain-text note. You type it the way you'd scribble it in Apple Notes:

Bench Press: 185 lbs 8, 8, 6
Incline DB Press: 70 lbs 10, 10, 9
Cable Row: 120 lbs 12, 12, 12

The colon marks the exercise, commas separate sets, and the text live-renders into cards with tappable weight and rep pills. To change a number mid-session you don't type at all: long-press it and slide — drag-to-dial — about two seconds a set, no keyboard. A plate mode steps through real plate loads with a haptic tick. Rest timers run as Lock Screen Live Activities, every exercise gets its own progress chart, and weight and rep PRs are detected automatically.

There's more under the hood — a Pro-tier AI coach that reads your actual training history and drafts workouts you approve before anything is saved, photo-based food logging with estimated calories and macros, body weight and cardio tracking — but the reason it takes the top slot is logging speed, because logging is the thing you do twenty times a session.

In Monolift Rebuilding your Strong program takes minutes, not an afternoon. Open a new note and type or paste each training day as plain text — "Squat: 225 lbs 5, 5, 5" — and it renders into cards ready to log. There's no automated Strong import, so your CSV stays as your history archive; the program itself moves as text.

The honest trade-offs: iPhone-only (iOS 17+), English-only, no Android, web, or Apple Watch app, and your old Strong history lives in your CSV rather than in Monolift's charts — your PR records start fresh. It's free to download with a 7-day free trial of everything, then a subscription; the App Store listing shows pricing.

2. Hevy — the closest like-for-like replacement

If you like how Strong works and just want off the ride, Hevy is the obvious move — obvious enough that we wrote a full Hevy vs Strong comparison. It uses the same structured logging model (routines, sets, a checkmark per set) plus a social feed where friends can see and like your workouts. Two things Strong switchers care about most: Hevy has a genuinely usable free tier — with limits on routines and custom exercises as of mid-2026 — and it imports Strong's CSV export directly, so your full history comes with you. That import alone makes it the easiest exit ramp.

The trade-off is the premise of this whole page: Hevy logs the same way Strong does. Tap, type, check. If pricing direction was your only complaint, Hevy solves it. If the flow was the problem, it won't — that crowd should read our Hevy alternatives guide too.

3. StrengthLog — the generous free tier

StrengthLog's free version does more than many trackers' paid tiers: unlimited workout logging, a few hundred exercises, 1RM calculators, and basic stats, with training programs and deeper analytics behind premium. It leans strength-sports — the exercise library and stats read like they were built by people who compete. The trade-offs: the interface is denser than Strong's, and logging is still structured field entry, so it's a value pick rather than a speed pick.

4. Boostcamp — if you'd rather follow a program

Boostcamp flips the model. Instead of a blank log, you pick from a large library of free coach-written programs and the app tracks you through them, with RPE/RIR logging, plate calculators, and rest timers. If your Strong frustration is actually programming frustration — you were logging fine but training aimlessly — this fixes the real problem. The trade-off: it's program-first by design, so if you write your own training, it's more scaffolding than you need.

Which Strong app alternative should you pick?

  • You want Strong, minus your complaint about Strong: Hevy. Your history imports, and the flow is nearly identical.
  • You want to spend nothing: StrengthLog's free tier will carry you a long way.
  • You want programming handled: Boostcamp.
  • You want no app at all: a spreadsheet or an Apple Notes template genuinely works — it's how half of us started.
  • You want logging to take two seconds a set: Monolift.

One honest note about starting fresh. Leaving your in-app history behind feels bigger than it is: the CSV keeps every number you'd ever actually look up, and any log worth using rebuilds your working numbers within a few sessions. The first weeks in a new tracker are mostly you re-setting PRs the app can see — which, if you're tracking progressive overload properly, is half the fun of switching.
